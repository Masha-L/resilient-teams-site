    The research

    31%of us are engaged at work.

    70%of that engagement is influenced by managers.

    If nearly 7 in 10 people are disengaged, and that's largely driven by their manager, burnout can't be the individual's problem to solve.

    ~ The core things ~

    The pyramid of greatness

    Whatever problem your team is solving, build it from the bottom up. Each layer holds up the next: strengths and motivations, growth mindset, team values, then mission, plan and success.

    Scroll to climb.

    ~ Step I · Who are we? ~

    Know thyself.

    Start with you: your team needs to respect you. You are human. You have strengths and weaknesses. You might have some experiences and lack others. That's okay.

    Your team will generally respond much better to you owning up to it than to you trying to fake it.

    Your quest · 7 minutes

    1. Name one top strength that feels especially true to you. How does it help you at work?
    2. Share your strengths with your neighbor. How would yours and theirs complement each other on a team?

    ~ Then: keep growing ~

    Growth mindset

    Growth mindset improves resilience, both at work and outside of it.

    Ask yourself

    What's one area where you'll build more growth mindset, and how can your team support you?

    Watch: Lydia Cheah on growth mindset

    ~ Step II · What do we care about? ~

    You have values.

    Your values are going to determine if your team can work for you.

    Values are your deeply held beliefs: the things you fall back on when you don't know how else to proceed.

    “It's not hard to make decisions when you know what your values are.”

    — Roy Disney

    Values are the shortcuts we use to make decisions when the manager isn't in the room.

    ~ Step II · What do we care about? ~

    Your values

    Your quest · 5 minutes

    1. Fill in the values worksheet. Trust your instincts; no list is perfect.
    2. Think of the last decision you made for your team. Was it consistent with your values?
    3. Compare with your neighbor. Where do your values overlap, and where would they complement each other?

    ~ Step III · Where are we going? ~

    Only now, point the team at your problem.

    • Mission What do we expect of ourselves and each other? Where should we be in a year? Two?
    • Plan Check in on what's working and what isn't. Run a Stop, Start, Continue retro, then rank ideas: Earth, Sky, Space.
    • Success Survey the team after, and revisit your values every few months.

    ~ Here be dragons ~

    But what if…

    • “What if my team hates it?”
    • “What if they think it's dumb?”
    • “What if we can't find common ground?”
    • “What if nobody says a word?”
    • “What if it surfaces a conflict I can't fix?”
    • “What if there's no time for this?”
    • “What if I'm not the right person to lead it?”
    • “What if nothing changes afterward?”

    Every lead has these. None of them is a reason not to start.

    ~ Whatever your problem ~

    Your playbook

    1. I

      Who are we?

      Understand how you can lead with your strengths and values, then learn your team's.

    2. II

      What do we care about?

      Write down your team's values and use them to make decisions.

    3. III

      Where are we going?

      Agree on the mission. Separate requirements from solutions.
